Procedure Note: Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)

Endoscope Model: EndoView 3000. Patient was placed in the left lateral decubitus position with a bite block for airway protection. The endoscope was introduced smoothly into the oropharynx.

Upon entering the esophagus, the mucosal surface appeared normal with no varices observed. The Z-line was clearly demarcated at 40 cm from the incisors. The gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) appeared with no stigmata of bleeding or erosion.

Transitioning into the stomach, the fundus and body were systematically inspected. A moderate degree of erythema was noted along the antrum with no ulcerations. Good insufflation demonstrated intact rugal folds. The pylorus appeared patent.

In the duodenum, the bulb and the second portion appeared normal, with visible villi and no evidence of ulceration or inflammation.

Retroflexion in the stomach allowed for a clear assessment of the cardia and fundus with no abnormalities noted.

Findings:

  • Esophagus: Normal Z-line at 40 cm, no signs of esophagitis.
  • Stomach: Moderate erythema in the antrum.
  • Duodenum: Normal appearance.

Impression:

This is a case of chronic dyspepsia with findings suggestive of gastritis without active ulceration.

Diagnosis:

Chronic gastritis; GERD

Interventions:

No biopsies were taken given the absence of suspicious lesions, and there were no interventions required.
