Procedure Note

Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)

The procedure commenced with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position. A flexible gastroscope (model FG-223) was utilized with a bite block placed to protect the instrument. Following satisfactory sedation, the endoscope was advanced through the oropharynx with minimal resistance. The upper esophageal sphincter was traversed without difficulty.

Within the esophagus, typical mucosal anatomical landmarks were observed. At 40 cm from the incisors, we noted the Z-line, and the gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) was demarcated without stigmata of reflux or varices. Only mild linear friable erythema was indicated.

Inspection of the stomach, entries were made to the fundus, body, and antrum. Moderate insufflation highlighted well-defined rugal folds without significant hiatus. There were scattered areas of patchy erythema in the antrum, compatible with mild gastritis. The pylorus was intubated successfully.

Observing the duodenum, circumferentially measured bulb walls appeared thickened and congested with minimal inflammatory changes noted in the second part.

A maneuver into retroflexion evaluated the gastric cardia and fundus, which were found to be unremarkable apart from inesquite hyperemic areas of the cardia region.

Findings:

  • Esophagus: Z-line at 40 cm without rings, mild erosive signs.
  • Stomach: Antral patchy erythema suggesting mild gastritis.
  • Duodenum: Mild duodenitis visualized with no ulceration.

Impression:

Findings consistent with mild erosive esophagitis and gastritis.

Diagnosis list:

  • Mild Reys Erosive Esophagitis
  • Slight Antral Gastritis

Interventions:

Eight cold biopsies obtained from the lower esophagus and antrum.

Procedure tolerated well.
Endoscopy team present: Dr. Sarah Edmunds (Endoscopist), Judy Redmond (RN, circulator), Mike Wilkens (Endoscopy Tech).
