Informed Consent: The patient has been informed of the procedure, risks,
and benefits and has provided informed consent.

The endoscope was introduced through the oropharynx without difficulty. The
upper esophageal sphincter was easily traversed. The esophagus appeared
normal with smooth mucosa, and the Z-line was visualized at 40 cm from the
incisors. No notable rings or strictures were observed, and no evidence of
esophagitis was present.

The examination of the stomach revealed a well-distended gastric body with
normal rugal folds. Mild erythema was noted in the antrum, but no erosions or
ulcers were observed. The pylorus was open and easily navigated.

In the duodenum, the bulb and second portion showed no mucosal
abnormalities. The villi appeared normal, with no signs of inflammation or
ulceration.

Retroflexion in the stomach was performed to assess the cardia and fundus,
revealing no abnormalities. The procedure was well-tolerated, and the patient
remained stable throughout.

Recovery Course: The patient experienced no pain or nausea during
recovery. Vital signs remained stable, and the patient tolerated oral liquids
without difficulty.
